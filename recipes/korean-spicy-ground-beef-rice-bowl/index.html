Korean Spicy Ground Beef Rice Bowl

Sweet, savoury and medium-spicy gochujang-glazed ground beef over fluffy rice, with crunchy cucumber, shredded carrot, green onions and sesame. Bulgogi-style flavour in an easy 30-minute dinner.

Ingredients

Beef and aromatics

  • 500 g ground beef
  • 1 tbsp neutral cooking oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated

Gochujang sauce

  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp gochujang (Korean chilli paste)
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp rice vinegar
  • 2 tbsp water

Rice and toppings

  • 2 cups cooked white rice
  • 1 cucumber, sliced
  • 1 carrot, shredded
  • 2 green onions, chopped
  • 1 tbsp sesame seeds
  • 2 eggs, optional

Directions

  1. Prepare the rice and toppings. Cook enough rice for 2 cups cooked, if not using leftovers. Slice 1 cucumber, shred 1 carrot and chop 2 green onions. Set aside 1 tbsp sesame seeds and 2 optional eggs.
  2. Mix the gochujang sauce. In a small bowl stir together 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp brown sugar, 1 tbsp gochujang, 1 tsp sesame oil, 1 tsp rice vinegar and 2 tbsp water until combined.
  3. Brown the beef. Heat 1 tbsp neutral oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add 500 g ground beef and cook for approximately 6–8 minutes, breaking it into small pieces, until browned and cooked to 71°C / 160°F. Drain excess fat if necessary.
  4. Add garlic and ginger. Add 3 minced garlic cloves and 1 tsp grated ginger to the skillet and stir for 30–60 seconds, until fragrant.
  5. Glaze the beef. Pour all of the prepared gochujang sauce into the skillet. Stir and simmer for 2–4 minutes until the beef is coated and the sauce is glossy. If it thickens too much, add a splash of water.
  6. Assemble the bowls. Divide 2 cups cooked rice between 2–3 bowls. Top with the spicy beef, sliced cucumber, shredded carrot, chopped green onions and sesame seeds. If using eggs, fry them separately and add one to each of two bowls.

Notes & variations

  • Heat level: Use ½ tbsp gochujang for mild, 1 tbsp for medium or 2 tbsp for hot. Gochujang brands vary in heat; taste and adjust.
  • Extra toppings: Try kimchi, a fried egg, a drizzle of Kewpie mayo or quick-pickled cucumber.
  • Meal prep: Keep cooked beef, rice and fresh vegetables in separate airtight containers. Refrigerate promptly and reheat the beef and rice thoroughly before serving.
  • Serving tip: This recipe makes 2 generous bowls or 3 smaller bowls.
